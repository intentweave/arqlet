# Where the time of a patch into a bus view goes (#212, 2026-11-27)

Measurement for [ADR 0029](../../docs/adr/0029-patch-into-bus-views.md) (proposed). No engine change.

- **Machine:** Apple M3 (8 cores, 16 GB), Darwin 25.0.0, rustc 1.99.0, Node 24.21.0. Shared machine: 1-minute load
  4.2 to 6.0 during the phase runs, 5.7 to 7.4 during the partial runs and 6.1 to 10.8 during the variant runs (the
  `load` field of every row). The times are rough; every comparison below is within one run, the requests in turn.
- **Revision:** `f59ec9f` (main, engine 0.10.0), clean. `layout-command` built with
  `CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4 cargo build --release --locked -p arqlet-runtime --bin layout-command`.
- **Probe build:** the same with `benchmarks/experiments/bus-patch-phases.patch` applied (nested wall-clock probes printed as
  `PHASE path ms count` on standard error, decision counters, and two environment switches read only by the probe:
  `ARQLET_PATCH_LOCAL=1` and `ARQLET_NO_SECOND_PASS=1`, see below). Without the switches its output is identical to main
  (same geometry hash for all 12 requests checked with both builds, rows labelled `main` and `probe`); its times are within
  the load noise of main.
- **Inputs:** `benchmarks/experiments/bus-mesh.mjs UNITS BUSES STRATEGY STYLE CHILDREN EDGES` (public, seeded). The
  overview is the graph without the children and their edges; the patch adds them in one `patch`; the fresh import
  imports the whole graph. One `layout-command` process per request, `timings: true`.
- **Driver:** `benchmarks/experiments/bus-patch.mjs` (time limit per process, one retry, rows saved per case);
  tables: `benchmarks/experiments/bus_patch_table.py`.

## 1. Where the time goes (probe build, medians of 3, ms)

pass" is the second routing pass of `compute_counted_inner` (`reroute` + `finish`, run when the first routing leaves 1 to
500 conflicts, #219), "of it overlaps" its `resolve_overlaps`; "bus drawing" is `bus_line::collapse` and
`bars_to_lines` (bars and taps drawn from the boxes); "rest" is JSON, projection and the remaining steps.

- **Dense view (100/30, 240 children, 1 000 edges):** 93 % of the patch is the second routing pass, 74 % its overlap
  resolution (8 rounds over every route). The first layout leaves 490 conflicts, just under the 500 that start the pass.
- **Large view (280/107, 480 children, 2 100 edges):** no second pass (1 954 conflicts after the first routing, over
  500). Placement 35 % (separation 23 %), first routing 37 %, drawing the bus lines 26 % (`bus_line::collapse`, the tap
  searches of #220).
- **Small organic view (60/12):** 86 % second pass, 59 % overlap resolution.
- **Layered views:** fast either way; the patch is *not* the fresh drawing (section 3).

## 2. What the patch keeps today, and why it equals a fresh import

`patch_snapshot` (`crates/runtime/src/patch.rs`) lays out an abstracting view (level of detail, communities, groups)
from scratch on purpose. Bus meshes are not abstracting, so it calls `arqlet_layout::compute(projected, Some(snapshot),
dirty = every node)`: a warm start in which every node is placed again, from its earlier box where it has one.

What the layout makes of it, by path (counters of the probe build):

- **Organic view with `busStyle: lines`: identical to a fresh import** (geometry hash equal on all three organic cases,
  "kept" 0 of 60/100/280 units). The decision is in the organic placement of `place` (`crates/layout/src/lib.rs`, the
  warm start of `Mode::Organic`): the area starts warm only when *every* child has an earlier box. The root's children
  include the bus nodes the layout adds (`bus_line::expand`), and their boxes do not come back:
  `bus_line::expand_snapshot` restores a bus node's box only for a bus that is still a bus line, but `bars_to_lines` has
  turned every organic bus into an explicit bus with edges from its head (its box sits in `bus_boxes`). So the root
  starts cold, the stress is deterministic, and every following step sees the input of a fresh import. The patch keeps
  nothing but the view state (collapsed containers, options, filter) and the bus roots (`bus_taps.roots`).
- **Organic view with `busStyle: bar`:** the bus boxes do come back, the root starts warm (counter
  `organicArea:warm=true`), and the drawing differs from a fresh import, and is much worse: 1 374 line–node hits against
  36, 1 731 conflicts against 56 (section 3). It is faster only because its first routing leaves over 500 conflicts, so
  the second pass is skipped.
- **Layered views:** the earlier plans are reused (`reused_plans`), and the hierarchical-ordering rebuild of an import
  (`previous.is_none()` in `compute_once`) does not run; the first routing leaves conflicts that a fresh import does not
  have (141 on 100/30 with 60 children). With many new edges it ends with 1 043 and 2 091 line–node hits where a fresh
  import has 0.

In no measured case does a patch reuse a route: `routesKept` is 0 everywhere. `local_routes` refuses because the earlier
drawing's graph is the view after `bars_to_lines` (edges from bus heads that the new expanded graph does not have), and
in the main cases the patch adds every edge of the view anyway (the overview has only bus taps).

row is the reference of the same run. Full rows: `bus-patch-variants-20261127.jsonl`, `bus-patch-partial-20261127.jsonl`.

- `local` (`ARQLET_PATCH_LOCAL=1`): only the added nodes and the containers above them are dirty, and the bus nodes get

- `nosecond` (`ARQLET_NO_SECOND_PASS=1`): the second routing pass is skipped (one end of #219).

## Findings

1. **A patch into these views costs a fresh import because it is one** (organic `lines`), or because it routes every
   edge again (all views). The time is in routing, not placement: the second routing pass with its overlap resolution
   (74 to 93 % on the views that get it), else the first routing and the bus-line drawing (37 % and 26 % on the large
   view). Placement and separation are at most 35 % (large view) and 2 to 3 % elsewhere.
2. **An incremental placement alone saves little and is not safe.** Holding the unchanged nodes (`local`) gives 3 to 22
   times the line–node hits of a fresh import when the patch brings most of the view's edges (better only on the
   smallest view: 19 against 24). When it brings a small part, it holds 279 of 280 and 100 of 100 units and the result
   is mixed: on the large view far fewer hits (311 against 1 408, better in the order of the objectives) with 1.8 times
   the shared lines; on the dense view 5 more hits (114 against 109, worse) with an eighth of the shared lines. Its time
   gain comes mostly from skipping the second pass when the first routing leaves over 500 conflicts, and from the
   cheaper placement on the large view (2.5 s against 3.6 s), never from kept routes: everything is routed again.
3. **The warm-start patch of today is already worse than a fresh import where it applies:** layered `lines` views
   (1 043 and 2 091 line–node hits against 0) and organic `bar` views (1 374 against 36). Only organic `lines` views
   escape it, by accident (the missing bus boxes). On the layered partial patch it is also 5.5 times slower (the second
   pass runs on its conflicts).
4. **Skipping the second pass is not free:** on the dense view 10 more line–node hits and 4.2 times the shared line
   pairs, for 9 times less time. The pass is what keeps shared lines down here; #219 must make it cheaper or bound it,
   not drop it.

## Not measured

- elk-rs: it has no buses, containers with organic areas or this task's options; the comparison would be on another
  task (the uniform pose of `benchmarks/eval`). Not run.
- Bars and taps through boxes in `bar` views (the bars are not routes): only the routes are scored. `lines` views score
  their taps as edges.
- Repeated runs for the variants (one run each, under load); thread counts (the layout of one view is serial) and WASM
  (no WASM build was run; the probes are native only).
- Larger views and real exports (only public generated views were used).
