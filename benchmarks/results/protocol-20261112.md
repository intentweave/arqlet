# Where the time of a request goes (ADR 0019, step 2, 2026-11-12)

Raw data: [`protocol-20261112T194137Z.jsonl`](protocol-20261112T194137Z.jsonl), written by
`python3 benchmarks/protocol/measure.py --sizes 10000 50000 100000 --reps 5 --max-load 2.5`.

Apple M3 (8 cores), macOS 26 (Darwin 25.0.0), rustc 1.99.0, Node 24.21.0, revision 62e90bd (clean). Load average 2.37
at the start and up to 3.65 during the runs (the runs themselves load the machine); medians of 5 runs after one warm-up,
single-threaded (`threads` 1). Graphs: `benchmarks/overview/generate.py --nodes N --seed 1` (directories, files,
symbols, calls). Requests: `import` with `initialCollapse: {depth: 1}` and `limit` 10 000 (the view limit), then on its
snapshot a `toggle` that opens the first collapsed container, `intent` (reads the snapshot and answers almost nothing:
what parsing a snapshot costs), `scene`, and `overview` of the whole graph.

The import keeps at most 10 000 selected nodes plus their ancestors (12 012 and 12 714 at 50 000 and 100 000); only the
overview processes the whole graph.

## The protocol's share in the browser path

Protocol work: stringify, copy, parsing the snapshot inside WASM (the `intent` time), parsing the answer, and the two
worker round trips; whole: all of that plus `lw_run`.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 000 | toggle | 664 | 156 | 23 % |
| 10 000 | scene | 118 | 102 | 87 % |

## Findings

1. **Reading and drawing are dominated by the protocol.** A scene costs 21–47 ms of work natively, but in the browser
   path 100–190 ms go to sending the snapshot (5.99 MB at 100 000 nodes) into the worker and parsing it in WASM. The same
   holds for `trace` and every other read. Keeping the state in the engine (ADR 0019, step 3) removes most of it.
2. **Edits are dominated by the layout.** A toggle spends 0.3–9.7 s in `handle`; the protocol is 2–23 %. The cost
   follows what the toggle brings into view: at 50 000 nodes it opens a container with 22 children (402 below), at
   100 000 one with a single child. This is layout work, not interface work, and needs its own look (what a toggle
   lays out again).
3. **Large inputs cost hundreds of milliseconds to move, not seconds.** The 17.5 MB graph of 100 000 nodes parses in
   100 ms natively; its worker round trip is 230 ms. Binary frames (step 5) are not the first lever.
4. **The overview is the slowest operation at 100 000 nodes:** 7.5 s natively and 7.9 s in WASM, single-threaded. Native
   builds can run it with more threads; WASM cannot yet.
5. **Snapshots stop growing with the graph** at about 6 MB here, because the import's view limit cuts the graph at
   10 000 selected nodes.
