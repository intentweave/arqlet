## Quality

`overview-d3.mjs --sizes 10000,50000 --reps 1` with the corrected measure → `overview-d3-20261111T115442Z.jsonl`. The
measures are deterministic (d3-force seeded), so the load does not change them; this run's times agree with the timed
run. Measured on the leaves, from their centres; edge length and cohesion in units of sqrt(area per leaf) of each
drawing's own bounding box.

| nodes | engine | overlaps (radius 4) | edge length | cohesion of files (lower: tighter) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 000 | Arqlet overview | 0 | 8.21 | 1.00 |
| 10 000 | d3-force | 0 | 3.77 | 7.36 |
| 50 000 | Arqlet overview | 0 | 16.24 | 1.01 |
| 50 000 | d3-force | 0 | 7.97 | 15.61 |

Arqlet keeps the symbols of a file 7 to 15 times closer together than d3-force; d3-force has edges about half as long.
That is the trade the hierarchy makes: an edge between two files or directories is long because the clusters keep
their members together, while d3-force pulls every edge short and lets clusters spread. Neither measure is a verdict
on its own.

The first run's `overlaps` column (`overview-d3-20261111T114711Z.jsonl`) scaled both drawings to the same area per leaf
before counting; that counted the dense packing inside Arqlet's clusters (and d3-force's) as overlaps and is wrong. The
script now counts in the engines' own coordinates.

## Reading

- The overview scales about linearly in this range (10 000 → 100 000 nodes: × 10.5 with one thread), and four threads
  are about three times as fast. WASM in one thread is close to the native single-thread time.
- d3-force lays out the same graphs 16 to 22 times slower here; it is not hierarchy-aware, so this compares the price
  of a flat force layout with the hierarchical one, not two implementations of one algorithm.
- Not measured: the browser (WebGL drawing, transfer to the worker), real data (an Intentweave export), graphs above
  100 000 nodes, and memory.
