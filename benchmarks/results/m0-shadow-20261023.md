# M0 shadow evaluation: key versus shadow score (2026-10-23)

Raw rows: `m0-shadow-20261023.jsonl` (one row per view, with environment and git revision).
Command per view: `bench --reps 1 --threads 1` on the 20 views of `benchmarks/run.sh` (threads 1 only; refinement output is thread-invariant).

## What was compared

For each view: the published conflict key of the imported input and of the refined result, and the shadow total of each (`scene::quality`, `QualityModel::shadow_default()`). The shadow score is reported only and does not select anything.

## Result

- Refinement improved the published key on all 20 views.
- The shadow total also rose on 19 of 20 views.
- The one disagreement is `workflow-dag 100`:
  - `sharedRoutePairs` 888 -> 33 (decides the key, which is lexicographic).
  - `routedCrossings` 42 -> 701 (about 16 times more crossings). Under the key it is only a lower-priority term, so it does not stop the change.
  - Shadow total falls by 54,009 millionths (about 0.05 points of the 0..100 scale).

## Reading this

- This is the tradeoff M0 is meant to expose. The lexicographic key accepts a large crossing increase in exchange for shared-route pairs. Whether crossings are a tier-1 constraint or a weighted term is a requirement decision (docs/adr/0002-quality-model.md), not something this evidence decides.
- The shadow model is not calibrated. Count terms use scale 1, so for raw counts in the hundreds the utility is close to zero and changes in them barely move the total. Several large changes (for example `sharedRoutePairs` 1613 -> 293) show little movement in the total. Scales need calibration on held-out graph families before the score can be trusted.
- Only accepted publications were compared. Rejected candidates are not logged, so this does not yet show how often the two orderings disagree during the search.

## Remaining M0 gaps

1. Candidate-level comparison (log rejected candidates too), not only published results.
2. Calibration of weights and scales on graph families not used for tuning.
3. Decision on which terms are tier 1 (hard), notably crossings and overlaps.

## Model v2 (tiers applied), same 20 views

Raw rows: `m0-shadow-v2-20261023.jsonl`. Model v1 above is kept as history.

- Tier 1 (hard violations: node overlaps and edge-node hits) is now counted apart from the weighted total, and a candidate with fewer violations ranks higher whatever its score.
- Every published result has zero hard violations on all 20 views, so tier 1 does not change any outcome here. The key and the tier order agree on 19 of 20 views.
- `workflow-dag 100` is still the one disagreement: tier 2 falls by 108,020 millionths while the key improves.
- Remaining gaps are unchanged: candidate-level comparison, and calibration of weights and scales on held-out families.

## Candidate level (2026-10-23)

Raw candidate rows and geometry: `candidates/` (one line per complete candidate; `geometryFile` for each non-duplicate). Logging does not change the result: `bench --candidate-log` checks this against the plain run on every view. The comparison is `candidate-compare-20261023.txt`, from `benchmarks/candidate_compare.py`.

- Measured candidates with distinct geometry, over the 20 views: 246 pairs with different keys.
  - Tune views (10, fixed by `calibration-split.json`): 124 pairs, 5 disagree.
  - Judge views (10): 122 pairs, 8 disagree.
- No pair involves a hard violation; each disagreement is decided by the weighted tier.
- In 12 of the 13 disagreements the key is decided by `nearNodeSegments` (hug pairs), not by crossings or bends. The shadow total weights hug pairs at scale 1, so differences of one or two pairs barely register.
- Largest gap: `er-diagram 100`, candidates 0 and 1. The shadow total prefers 0 by about 9 points, while the key prefers 1 by a single near-node difference.
- Nothing was fitted. The split and the rule were fixed before the comparison. Each disagreement is listed with its geometry for visual inspection, which is the next step before any calibration.

## Review of the disagreements (2026-10-23)

- Pattern: 12 of the 13 disagreements are the same trade-off. The key prefers fewer near-node pairs (`nearNodeSegments`) and accepts more routed crossings. The shadow prefers fewer crossings (weight 3) and gives near-node pairs weight 1. The thirteenth (`synthetic-2k 100`) is the same trade with shared-route pairs instead of near-node pairs.
- Example, `er-diagram 100` #0 vs #1 (rendered for review): #1 has 2 more crossings (3 vs 1), 1 fewer near-node pair and 3 fewer bends. The key takes #1 at the near-node position. The shadow total takes #0 by about 9.2 points, which is 3 x (1/2 - 1/4) / 8 x 100 from the crossing term alone.
- Both tune and judge views show the same pattern, so it is not an artefact of the split.
- Conclusion: the disagreement is about the priority of proximity against crossings. It is a requirement question, not a scoring error. The key and the score are both self-consistent. The decision on crossings vs near-node proximity belongs to ADR 0002.
