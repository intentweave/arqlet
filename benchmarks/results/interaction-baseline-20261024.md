# Interaction baseline: full recomputation per edit, drag, six scenes and large variants

Machine: Darwin arm64, 16 GiB (Apple silicon, not idle-controlled). Revision: `a83fcbc`. Build: `cargo build --release`, example `interaction_baseline`, scenes `benchmarks/scenes/*.json` (generated by `benchmarks/scenes/generate.py`). Each figure: median of 5 runs, wall clock, one process. Treat the figures as orders of magnitude, not as a result.

What is measured:
- Operations as full recomputations of the view: import, resize (`measure`), root routing, root direction, root strategy. Refusals are results and carry their reasons.
- A drag of the first leaf, as move-node steps (`crates/runtime`, `move_node`): begin, four updates, finish keeping the pin, begin and finish releasing it. Each step is a full recomputation; `finish-keep` with no change of the pins reuses the snapshot.
- Stability: the largest number of unrelated nodes whose boxes changed during the drag.

Not measured (not implemented yet):
- The pointer-to-preview time: the browser preview does not exist yet; its time cannot be measured from the engine.
- Coalescing and discarding of updates: the host's job; the engine is only asked the step it is given.
- Spatial index and local repair: not built.

Reading: a drag costs about one full recomputation per update, so a pointer at 60 Hz with a 450-node view cannot be served by full recomputation alone; the preview and a bounded update rate are needed. The stability column shows the layout re-arranges unrelated nodes; a local repair would have to keep them fixed.
