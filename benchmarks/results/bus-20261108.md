# Explicit buses, 2026-11-08

Branch `feature/bus-routing` on `7cedf82` plus the backbone and scene changes, Apple M3, release build of `layout-command`, default single pass.
Raw: [bus-20261108.json](bus-20261108.json). Command: `node benchmarks/eval/bus.mjs --out …` (from `benchmarks/eval`).
Deterministic; no times are claimed.

## Task

32 corpus views (150-node cut). `can-bus` brings its own two buses (a CAN backbone and a fan of wired outputs); in every other
view each node's outgoing edges (two or more, no self-loops) form one explicit bus (658 buses, 1630 member edges). Each view is
laid out without and with the buses in the graph and measured by the same `metrics` operation, which counts no shared line,
near-parallel run or crossing between two members of one bus. "Ink" is the length of all lines with shared stretches counted once.
`layerweave-code` was regenerated by a playground build before this run (201 nodes, 294 edges in the cut); both arms use the
same graph.

of its own, and the ECUs are ordered around it. Drawn by the drawing step alone (before amendment B) the same graph had ink 5030
and also no crossing; the rank of its own costs some length and gives the bus a place the layout and a later label can use.
Every ECU and every component on the bus has one stub; the messages between components keep their way inside their ECUs. It is
laid out by the pipeline before single pass (the engine ECU's straight routing; reported). (`layerweave-code` is regenerated by
every playground build, so the sums differ between runs; both arms of one run use the same graph.)

17 views change; the other 15 have no axis-parallel routes (organic, tree, polyline and mixed views), and their buses are
reported as not drawn. Largest ink savings: deployment-cluster −20 %, network-vlan −16 %, activity-order −13 % (with 10 fewer

A bus does not move nodes or other edges and never adds a hit or a shared line with an edge outside it: a member whose shared
way would enter a node or share a line keeps its own route (471 `Bus …` diagnostics in all, most of them the non-orthogonal
views).

## How a bus is laid out and drawn (`crates/layout/src/bus_layout.rs`, `bus.rs`)

A backbone (several sources, several targets, no node on both sides) is first a virtual node of the layout (40 × 8 across the
flow) in the deepest area holding its members' ends; every member runs through it. After the layout the canonical graph comes
back, the node's box is kept in `busBoxes`, and the drawing step below draws the members on a line through it.


On the finished routes. A bus whose edges have several sources and several targets is a backbone: one line across the channel
every member passes (after the last source, before the first target), at a member corner there or the channel's middle, chosen
by the same score; each node's members share its middle port, so each node has one stub. For a bus whose edges share an end: the members that leave (enter) that node along the same axis and
side get one port (the middle port of the members that run straight from the node, so they stay straight) and one way across
the first channel (the trunk), chosen among the members' first corners by: most members kept on the bus, fewest crossings with
edges outside it, fewest bends, nearest the node. Each member then branches off onto the lane its own route took.
