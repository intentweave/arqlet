# Layered construction: backward edges, port order, crossing cost, nested self-loops, 2026-10-31

Machine: Apple M3, 8 logical CPUs, macOS 25.0.0, release, bench with `--features benchmark-timings`. Revision ac137e6 plus
uncommitted changes (all behind `layeredConstruction`; default output unchanged, 16 of 16 refinement fingerprints).
`bench --reps 3 --threads 1`, medians; limit 100 (er-diagram and tree-100: 1000). Raw rows: `layered-crossings-20261031.jsonl`
(the option's rows, then the existing path for state-tcp, deployment-cluster and self-loops). Compared with
`all-datasets-off-20261031.jsonl` (existing path) and `joint-order-20261031.jsonl` (029b5e0). The machine was not idle.

Dataset note: `benchmarks/datasets/` is regenerated by `apps/playground/build.sh`, and `layerweave-code` is built from the
repository's own source. A playground build at 02:26 grew it from 669 nodes / 1084 edges to 714 / 1233 and made the
construction look five times slower. All layerweave-code rows here use the graph generated from 0bcfd56 (669 / 1084,
sha256 a7636dbb…), which reproduces the stored existing-path row exactly (geometry fingerprint 5119063bfec55801,
2,251,396 grid pops, 1327 crossings).

Changes:
1. Backward edges, as in the layered method's cycle break: a backward link takes part in the rank order reversed and gets
   dummies when it skips ranks; its ports lie on the reversed flow sides; the constructor builds it from target to source
   and reverses the points, so the route keeps the logical direction.
2. Outer lanes reach 16 tracks beyond the outermost box (was 3): long backward edges found no free track above a row.
3. Ports follow the ways: the constructed edges of one node side take their port slots in the order of their first or
   last lane change; the published ports take the ends of the routes. An edge whose port moved and that then fails is
   left out of the reordering and the construction is repeated (up to four rounds, then without reordering), so no two
   edges end in one point.
4. Crossing cost in the constructor's search: every crossing with a route already built costs more than any length, in
   the choice of the way and of the tracks.
5. Self-loops of a node with ports on two adjacent sides are nested around the corner between them.
6. Search speed (same output, same fingerprint): the pieces of a lane change between neighbouring lanes are tested once
   per corridor instead of every span from every lane, and crossings are counted by binary search in per-column and
   per-lane lists.
