# Columnar scene and overview against JSON (ADR 0019, step 4, 2026-11-18)

Raw data: [`columns-20261118T140709Z.jsonl`](columns-20261118T140709Z.jsonl), written by
`MAXLOAD=4 WAIT=600 sh benchmarks/protocol/measure-columns.sh 5` (`benchmarks/protocol/measure-columns.mjs`).

Apple M3 (8 cores), Darwin 25.0.0, rustc 1.99.0, Node 24.21.0, revision 9f2b5e0 with uncommitted changes (the columns for
ports, labels, rows and conflicts and this script's counting; the built WASM contained them). Load average 4 at the start
and 2 at the end: quieter than the earlier run but not idle. Medians of 5 runs after one warm-up (3 for the overview), one
run per size. Graphs: `benchmarks/overview/generate.py --seed 1` at 10 000 and 100 000 nodes, import limit 10 000.

An earlier run the same day (load 17, and counting only the node and route ids among the strings) said 8–9 times smaller;
that was wrong because the label and kind tables were missing from the column side. It is replaced by this one.

Path measured: the engine call in WASM (Node), then the answer to the page through a worker (Node `worker_threads`).
JSON: parsed, then structured-cloned. Columns: copied once out of the module's memory, transferred. "Views" is a host that
calls the engine directly and reads typed-array views (nothing copied, nothing moved). Column bytes are the typed arrays plus
the JSON of everything else in the answer (id, label, text and kind tables, extent, buses).

## Scene of a resident session

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 000 | depth 1, one container opened | 107 / 781 | 19.8 | 2.3 | 1.7 | 571 030 | 172 216 (126 584 + 45 632) |
| 100 000 | depth 1, one container opened | 521 / 8 570 | 264.7 | 32.3 | 28.7 | 7 029 003 | 2 067 237 (1 546 452 + 520 785) |
| 10 000 | full view, no abstraction | 10 000 / 15 111 | 760.5 | 405.5 | 398.2 | 12 672 694 | 3 525 767 (2 502 300 + 1 023 467) |
| 100 000 | full view, no abstraction | 12 714 / 11 831 | 900.6 | 501.5 | 492.2 | 12 967 102 | 3 513 723 (2 583 972 + 929 751) |

- **Size:** columns are 3.3 to 3.7 times smaller than the JSON scene. The typed arrays alone are about a fifth of the JSON; the
  strings (ids, labels, kinds, the same text as in the JSON) are about a quarter of the column side (25–29 %) and cap the gain.
- **Time:** where the view is small the transfer dominates and columns are 8.2 to 8.6 times faster (19.8 → 2.3 ms,
  265 → 32 ms). In the full views the time is mostly the engine building the scene (`build_scene`, which both paths do) and
  columns are 1.8 to 1.9 times faster. Without the worker (views) the numbers are about the same, so the gain is the
  missing serialization and parsing, not the transfer.
- What is not measured: a renderer reading the columns, Chrome. These graphs have no ports, labels, rows or conflicts in
  their scene; the columns for them are tested for equality with the scene, not measured.

## Overview (a session with a resident graph)

| nodes | request | JSON arrays (ms) | typed arrays, transferred (ms) | JSON bytes | typed bytes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 000 | whole answer for a new graph | 761.4 | 741.7 | 692 849 | 865 963 |
| 10 000 | continuation (`positionsOnly`) | 9.6 | 5.6 | 183 066 | 245 702 |
| 100 000 | whole answer for a new graph | 7 774.4 | 7 670.1 | 7 749 501 | 8 789 377 |
| 100 000 | continuation (`positionsOnly`) | 111.0 | 63.6 | 1 917 068 | 2 454 307 |

- **The whole answer is dominated by the layout** (seconds at 100 000 nodes), identical in both modes; 761 vs 742 ms and
  7 774 vs 7 670 ms are within noise. Nothing here supports a claim for the whole answer.
- **A continuation** is 1.7 times faster with typed arrays at both sizes (9.6 → 5.6 ms, 111 → 63.6 ms), the case that
  matters for a live view (every settle step after a drop). Its layout work is the same in both modes, so the difference is
  the missing serialization and parsing of three arrays.
- **The typed answer is larger, not smaller** (+25 % whole, +34 % continuation): the JSON positions are rounded to
  hundredths and short, small integers are as short in JSON as in binary, while the columns hold `f64` (positions) and
  4-byte integers. Size is therefore no argument for columns of the overview; the gain is time. `f64` is kept so that a
  viewer finds the same chains in both (a test compares models and vertex buffers); `f32` would halve the positions but
  can merge discs 0.01 apart beyond about 1.3 · 10^5. Positions as `i32` hundredths would be exact and about a third
  smaller than the JSON; that is a candidate for the binary frames of step 5, where bytes matter (over a network), not
  here, where the buffers are transferred without a copy.

## In Chrome (the cluster overview page)

`node apps/playground/browser-checks/overview-columns.mjs <reps> <source>` drives `overview.html` in a headless Chrome
(the WASM worker) with the box "typed arrays" off and on. Apple M3, load 5 to 9 (not quiet), Chrome headless, one run, so
the times are indications.

- **The picture is the same:** screenshots of the stage are byte for byte equal with JSON arrays and with typed arrays, after
  a fresh layout and after "Lay out again" (10 000 and 50 000 nodes); the model's numbers are `Float64Array` with the box on
  and `Array` with it off. So the viewer draws from the typed arrays as it does from JSON.
- **Times the page reports** (layout, transfer and receiving; "Lay out again" is a layout from scratch on the same
  clusters, not a settle continuation):

| source | JSON arrays: fresh / again (s) | typed arrays: fresh / again (s) |
|---|---|---|
| code-10000 (3 runs again) | 0.77 / 0.68, 0.69, 0.70 | 0.72 / 0.68, 0.69, 0.70 |
| code-50000 (2 runs again) | 4.01 / 3.68, 4.10 | 3.87 / 3.35, 3.41 |

  At 10 000 nodes the layout dominates and there is no difference. At 50 000 the typed arrays are 5–17 % faster in these
  few runs, which fits a saving of a few tenths of a second of serialization but is not established. The case where columns
  matter most, a settle continuation after a drag (ms, not seconds), is not driven by this page check; its engine side is in
  the table above (1.7×).

## Drawing the scene with the SVG viewer in Chrome

[`scene-draw-20261118T171337Z.jsonl`](scene-draw-20261118T171337Z.jsonl), written by
`WAIT=900 MAXLOAD=4 node apps/playground/browser-checks/scene-draw.mjs 5 code-10000`. Apple M3, headless Chrome 157, Node
24.21.0, revision a49f8de plus uncommitted changes (the viewer takes columns); the 10 000-node graph of the earlier tables,
import limit 10 000. **Load average 18.7: the script waited 15 minutes for a load of 4 and the machine never got there.**
Medians of 5 runs after one warm-up, main thread with real DOM, engine called directly (no worker). "To paint" is the time
until the second animation frame after `renderSvg`.

| view | path | engine (ms) | `renderSvg` (ms) | to paint (ms) | total (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 107 boxes, 781 routes (7 159 elements) | JSON scene | 15.6 | 9.5 | 24.5 | 47.7 |
| | columns | 3.4 | 11.8 | 17.8 | 33.0 |
| 10 000 boxes, 15 111 routes (158 249 elements) | JSON scene | 616.8 | 257.1 | 291.8 | 1 115.4 |
| | columns | 455.9 | 256.6 | 297.2 | 1 044.1 |

Parts alone: `sceneOfColumns` takes 0.4 ms (small view) and 6.7 ms (10 000 boxes); `renderSvg` on a scene object made
beforehand takes 11.4 and 217.2 ms.

- **The saving is the engine call**, as in the transfer measurements (15.6 → 3.4 ms, 617 → 456 ms): no scene JSON to build,
  stringify and parse. The page's total is 1.4 times smaller in the small view and 7 % in the large one.
- **Drawing is DOM work and does not change.** `renderSvg` takes the same time from columns as from the scene object (the
  differences are noise at this load), and building 158 000 elements plus style, layout and paint is 550 ms of the 1 044.
- **The conversion is not what to optimize:** turning the columns into the scene object costs 0.4 ms and 6.7 ms, under 1 %
  of the draw in the large view. Reading the columns by index inside `renderSvg` would save at most that, so the viewer
  keeps its code and takes columns through `sceneOfColumns`. What would help the large view is drawing fewer elements
  (level of detail, canvas or WebGL like the overview), not another data format.
- Both paths draw the same number of elements (checked in the page); the markup from columns equals the scene's with
  `f32` coordinates in the viewer's tests (`packages/viewer/scene-columns.test.mjs`).
